FEATURED CASE STUDY / 02

WEIRD WEST.

Quest design, recurring character encounters and a surprisingly intricate one-card poker game.

2022QUEST DESIGNNARRATIVE & WRITINGGAMEPLAY SCRIPTING
01 / OVERVIEW

In the Weird West, one has to wear many hats.

I worked remotely with WolfEye Studios for approximately a year and a half as a contract Level Designer. At the time, the roughly 25-person studio was entirely remote and contractor-based, with a culture that encouraged everyone to contribute beyond their formal role.

Because Weird West is an immersive sim/RPG with procedurally generated environments and systemic encounters, much of my day-to-day work was closer to quest design than conventional level construction. I also contributed scripting, some art and a substantial amount of writing.

02 / QUEST DESIGN

Building the optional world.

For a period during development, I acted as the de facto side-quest lead, coordinating two other quest designers/scripters. This was a practical responsibility, not a formal credited title.

Together we created and maintained a body of optional quests and mission-focused encounters intended to make the world feel worth exploring beyond its central storylines.

MY CONTRIBUTIONSQuest design · Content coordination · Gameplay scripting · Dialogue writing
03 / NARRATIVE DESIGN

The Heathen: a thread across stories.

Weird West follows different protagonists through interconnected journeys. The Heathen is a recurring character who helps tie those stories together, appearing in a mandatory encounter in each storyline and in additional random encounters.

I conceived and wrote, in whole or in part, most of these encounters. Each needed to work as a satisfying interaction in its own right while contributing to the character’s larger presence across the game.

For me, this was narrative design at its most interesting: character writing expressed through playable encounters, rather than separated from gameplay.

04 / SYSTEMS DESIGN

One-card poker: keep the tension, lose the complexity.

No Western feels complete without poker. An early attempt to reproduce conventional poker already existed when I joined, but the rules and implementation were inconsistent. A fully featured simulation was beyond the available time and resources.

Instead, I redesigned it as a single-card game that retained betting, uncertainty and relative card strength. After an ante, each side received one card. The player could fold, check or raise; scripted opponent logic responded using its hidden card and the player’s action. After two rounds of betting, the cards were compared to determine the winner.

There were no rendered cards and no dedicated card interface. Everything was conveyed through printed dialogue, which meant gameplay scripting, opponent decisions and writing all had to operate together.

“Preserve the experience, not necessarily the original mechanics.”
05 / SHIPPED FEATURE

A game within the game.

The completed poker mechanic shipped in Weird West as a repeatable, optional activity. On entering a town or certain other locations, players might encounter someone sitting at a table waiting for an opponent. If they had enough money, they could sit down and play.

The result was a self-contained system that fit the world, invited repeat engagement and stayed achievable within production constraints.
